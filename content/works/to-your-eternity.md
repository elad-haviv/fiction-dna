## To Your Eternity

**DNA sentence:** A being without a human identity acquires one through attachment, loss, and the responsibility of carrying traces of others forward.

**Premise anchor.** The story begins with an unusual wolf and a lonely boy and develops around life, death, reincarnation, and love.

**Emotional promise and tension.** The central experience is learning personhood from the outside. An enduring being can accumulate experiences without automatically understanding how to value or respond to them. Continuity of existence does not make separation easy.

**Story engine and world.** Encounter a person or community, acquire a capacity or understanding through contact, suffer a change in that relationship, and carry its consequences into another situation. Fushi's transformations make influence unusually literal: other lives affect what the protagonist can become. The people encountered must remain specific enough to be more than steps in a curriculum.

**Relationships and delivery.** Care often arrives before understanding. Being fed, named, sheltered, or recognized establishes personhood through interaction. A shifting cast and larger spans of time create tension between memory and the need to form new attachments. Repeated forms and gestures can hold the presence of an absent person without recreating the original relationship.

**Why it may matter to you.** You may respond to identity as something relational rather than self-generated, and to the thought that another person's influence survives in how someone acts. This overlaps with Frieren but begins from a much more radical lack of human experience.

**Reusable technique.** Make relationships leave durable changes in capability, habit, language, or ethical judgment. Reuse those changes later in a situation that requires interpreting the inherited lesson rather than mechanically repeating it.

**Failure mode.** Introducing characters only to destroy them for emotional fuel, or treating immortality as automatically profound. Repeated loss needs changing meaning, and later supernatural possibilities should not be described by a simplistic “nothing ever returns” rule.

**Tags:** acquired personhood; relational identity; memory; care; enduring attachment.
